# Ruler of Everything

Time is an intriguing concept. Mechanical hands tick, tick, and tick continuously on a wall, subtly ruling everything, while we stand powerless beneath them. The [song](/music/ruler-of-everything-unknown-artist) playing with this post gets me thinking about that, so I wanted to share some thoughts about time.

The weird thing is, we never stop trying to fight back. We're designed to survive, so we shoot at the sun[^1], desperate to conquer what we can't even touch.

To know what lies ahead is precious. To change what's ahead? Priceless.

# Why Do We Love The Past so Much?

I believe we reminisce about the past not because we miss it, but because there's comfort in knowing what comes after it. When we look at the past, sweet or sour, it feels safe because the plot has already played out and the dust has settled.

Fear exists only because of uncertainty, and because we can't bear that void, we invent "meaning" and "purpose" out of thin air, it's not just when it comes to the future, but it applies here especially. It's just our brain's desperate attempt to manufacture certainty where none exists, pretending we are steering the ship when the current decided our route before Obama was president ( that feels like an eternity ago to be honest ).

That’s why we cling to narratives and chase lifelong goals. We chase our lives like a quest with a happy ending, forgetting to admit that the unknown ahead is completely indifferent to us.

# What's [Over The Garden Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Garden_Wall), Greg?

I like to think about it in reverse. Let's be probabilistic and realistic for the sake of this thought experiment.

### Epilogue

Picture your last day alive. You're probably lying in a quiet room somewhere in your seventies, dying of sickness or old age. What is actually running through your head in those final weeks or minutes? Do you care about your old reputation? Your savings? The arguments you won?

I doubt you would. I know I wouldn't. The clock winds down, and all that frantic energy just evaporates. It's like sitting in an empty theater while the credits scroll past, waiting for an after-credits scene that might, or never was filmed.

The funny thing is that death isn't even that tragic, it's the moment the ruler of everything finally stops mocking your ambition, with nothing but total silence and zero consequences left to care about.

### Resolution

Let's wind it back to your 50s and 60s. You've retired. The noise of life slowly fades, and the frantic need to prove your existence finally burns out. You aren't looking to be successful anymore, you're just looking for that cup of coffee, because you worked your whole life after all, you need a bit of rest.

You feel the urge to live the life you missed busy chasing whatever you were chasing, but you realize it's a bit late, you're no longer interested in what younger you wanted. Realization hits that the life younger you was chasing to achieve never arrived, or at least, current you doesn't really want it anymore.

So all you desire now is a quiet afternoon, and making sure the people you care about don't stumble as hard as you did. The illusion of what once was ambitious crumbles into something small, quiet, and as much as you'll hate to say it, bland.

### Climax

You're in your 30s to 40s. The absolute peak of the performance. This is where the panic peaks because this is where the stakes feel absolute. Money, job commitments, raising kids, etc. Everything is like a matter of life and death because this is when you make things matter.

You spend your waking hours screaming into the machinery. You're so busy keeping up that you don't even notice the mechanical hands nudging you closer to the exit, one tick at a time.

### Rising Action

Wind it back to your early 20s. You're full of life and ambition, but you're scared shitless of too much sky. You think too much, so you try to do too much.

You obsess over whether you picked the wrong major, the wrong career, or the wrong friends, convinced that one misstep right now will ruin the entire plot thirty years down the line. After all, you want perfect, and perfect can't start sloppy now can it?

You want freedom, but the dizzying weight of having all options open is paralyzing. You are terrified simply because the canvas is completely blank, and you think you have to paint a masterpiece on your first try.

### Prologue

The present. It's not technically the prologue of your life, but it's the closest you can go back to right now.

It is the raw, unedited baseline where you are standing today. You're caught in between the comfort of who you were and the terror of who you're supposed to become, staring at the clock and wondering if you're already running late.

# My Point?

Look at how you picture yourself 5 or 10 years from now.

You think about that future version of you as if they hold the key to some grand revelation. You tell yourself, _once I get through these years, once I hit that promotion, once I move out, once I fix my routine, everything will finally make sense._

You're thinking about it completely wrong.

Strip away the details, and life follows a formula. Billions of people have stood right where you are standing, convinced their specific anxieties were unprecedented, only to walk the exact same conveyor belt slumbered into the same [amazon](https://www.amazon.com) delivery box into death. The mechanical hands don't write unique endings; they churn out the same script over and over.

> _"I know you I've been you, your facade is a scam."_[^2]

So just live it. Take the trip, make the mess, sit with the people you love, and enjoy the absurdity of the whole ride while you actually have the legs to run it. Don't think about it, [just do it](https://www.nike.com) ( nike should pay me)
